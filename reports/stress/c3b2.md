# c3b2: escalation, tiling and the text layer (2026-11-08)

Three general changes, aimed at the limits found in `README.md` (dense pages, tiny print, misread rare words):

1. **Visibly failed pages are always re-read**, whatever the 20% / 10% budgets. A page qualifies when the
   reading:
   - hit the output cap,
   - was ≥ 7,000 tokens long ("dense"),
   - is mostly repetition, or
   - (born-digital PDFs) has < 60% of its words in the page's own text layer.

   Code: `docengine/reread.py`, `must_reread`.
2. **Dense pages are read in tiles.** Truncated or dense pages are cut along the layout model's regions (same
   column, ≤ ¼ page each), rendered at 400 dpi, read separately and joined in reading order. Code:
   `docengine/tiles.py`. A whole-page re-read gets one retry; if it still loops, it is tiled.
3. **Text-layer reference** (born-digital PDFs only). A word the reader got almost right takes the PDF's exact
   spelling when it aligns 1:1 between matching neighbours. Structure, maths, table numbers and reading order stay
   the reader's. Code: `docengine/textlayer.py`. Guards against layer defects:
   - invisible or under-image OCR layers,
   - custom-encoding junk,
   - missing spaces, lost ligatures,
   - broken accents, flattened sub/superscripts.

   If our reading is nearly empty while the layer is clean, the layer becomes the text.

## Dense, tiny-text pages (word recall %, avg over 1–3 columns; 54 pages with exact text)

| text | font | c3b (before) | **c3b2** | Infinity-Pro on every page |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| English | 3 pt (8–10k words) | 34 | **98** | 88 |
| English | 3.5 pt | 68 | **98** | 98 |
| English | 4 pt | 66 | **99** | 98 |
| English | 4.5–10 pt | 96–99 | **98–99** | 98–99 |
| random words + numbers | 3 pt | 0 | **90** | 49 |
| random words + numbers | 3.5 pt | 1 | **97** | 55 |
| random words + numbers | 4 pt | 33 | **98** | 58 |
| random words + numbers | 4.5 pt | 32 | **97** | 95 |
| random words + numbers | 5 pt | 63 | **100** | 97 |
| random words + numbers | 6 pt | 85 | **97** | 98 |
| random words + numbers | 7–10 pt | 94–98 | **99** | 99 |

Where it comes from (first c3b2 run, before the dense trigger):
- Tiling + escalation alone lift English at 3 pt from 34 → 98 and random words at 3–4 pt from 0–33 → 74–91.
- The text layer then adds 5–23 points on rare words, which the readers "correct" into common ones.

The text layer only helps born-digital PDFs. On scans, the tiling numbers (before the text layer) are the ones
that apply.

## Normal documents: nothing breaks

- **olmOCR-Bench: 86.9 ± 0.9** (c3b 87.2 ± 0.8). Changes by cause:

  | cause | tests fixed | tests broken |
  |---|---|---|
  | text-layer corrections | 6 | 1 |
  | escalation re-reads (21 pages) | 0 | 3 |
  | pages processed identically: run-to-run sampling (LightOnOCR temperature 0.1) | 1 | 14 |

  Of the 14 sampling breaks, 12 come from one old maths scan whose stage-2 reading came out differently.

  The same text-layer step applied offline to the c3b outputs gives 87.3 (0 broken, 6 fixed).
- **200 random web PDFs**: precision / recall against their text layers 96.2 / 92.4 (c3b 96.3 / 92.5). Six pages were
  escalated and 2 were tiled.
- **Extra pages re-read**: olmOCR-Bench 15 outside budget (of 1,403), random web 3 (of 200), stress 26 (of 54).

## Cost and time

Stage 1 was reused (`--from-run`). On olmOCR-Bench, stage 2 took 1,172 s vs 455 s before. A handful of escalated
pages each looped to the 12k-token cap 3 times, and the slowest page sets the wall time. Re-read attempts are now
capped at 2 (then tiles), which brought the stress run's stage 2 from 1,057 s to 729 s. The text-layer step costs
14–180 s of CPU.

## Still open

- **Fonts that do not render** (a Hebrew web page drawn as empty boxes) are still wrong. The readers invent text,
  the font is embedded so it can't be flagged that way, and character statistics can't tell it apart from table
  pages. The text layer is right, but there is no safe signal yet to switch to it.
- **Scans have no text layer.** For them, small rare words at 5–6 pt stay the weak spot (85–97%).
